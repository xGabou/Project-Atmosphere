## 3. Current Pressure System Map

Pressure is generated as forecast/base pressure, copied into live regional state, then adjusted by several live systems:

- Forecast/base pressure: generated by `PressureGenerator`, stored on each `ForecastRegion`, then used to create `RegionAtmosphereState`.
- Live pressure: stored in `RegionAtmosphereState.pressure`, clamped to `900..1080 hPa`, and persisted by `AtmosphericStateSavedData`.
- Target pressure: `RegionAtmosphereState.getTargetPressure(dayTime)` interpolates the immutable daily forecast pressure profile.
- Scheduler recovery: `AtmosphericUpdateScheduler` moves live pressure toward target pressure and base pressure.
- Rain pressure recovery: scheduler raises pressure while rain intensity exists.
- Guard correction: scheduler applies a stronger correction when live pressure deviates more than the guard threshold from target pressure.
- Seasonal pressure drift: `SeasonalAtmosphericDrift` nudges pressure toward forecast target plus seasonal pressure offset.
- Cyclone forcing: `CycloneManager` lowers pressure inside active cyclone influence, capped by allowed deficit.
- Ocean basin pressure coupling: `OceanBasinManager` uses `AtmosphereFluxInfluence` to move coastal/ocean-influenced regions toward basin pressure memory.
- Wind mixing: `WindVector.update` moves pressure between active neighboring regions.
- WeatherCell feedback: WeatherCells do not directly write pressure, but low pressure contributes to support scores.

## 4. Systems That Raise Pressure

- `AtmosphericUpdateScheduler` rain pressure term: `rainIntensity * PRESSURE_RESTORE * updateScale`, positive only.
- `AtmosphericUpdateScheduler` forecast recovery: raises pressure when live pressure is below target pressure.
- `AtmosphericUpdateScheduler` pressure guard: raises pressure when live pressure is more than the guard threshold below target pressure.
- `RegionAtmosphereState.relaxTemperatureAndPressureTowardBase`: raises pressure when live pressure is below region base pressure.
- `SeasonalAtmosphericDrift`: can raise pressure in seasons with positive pressure offset, especially winter/autumn.
- `OceanBasinManager` pressure coupling: can raise pressure if basin target pressure is above live pressure.
- `WindVector.update`: can raise pressure if neighboring active regions have higher pressure.

## 5. Systems That Lower Pressure

- `AtmosphericUpdateScheduler` forecast recovery: lowers pressure when live pressure is above target pressure.
- `AtmosphericUpdateScheduler` pressure guard: lowers pressure when live pressure is far above target pressure.
- `RegionAtmosphereState.relaxTemperatureAndPressureTowardBase`: lowers pressure when live pressure is above base pressure.
- `SeasonalAtmosphericDrift`: can lower pressure in seasons with negative pressure offset, especially summer/spring.
- `CycloneManager`: lowers pressure inside active cyclone influence.
- `OceanBasinManager` pressure coupling: can lower pressure if basin target pressure is below live pressure.
- `WindVector.update`: can lower pressure if neighboring active regions have lower pressure.

## 6. Systems That Move Or Diffuse Pressure

- `WindVector.update` is the direct region-to-region pressure mixing path. It samples active regions and neighbors, computes `(neighborPressure - pressure) * pressureFactor`, accumulates it, and clamps total pressure movement to `+/-6 hPa` per wind update.
- `OceanBasinManager` moves pressure between ocean basin memory and influenced atmosphere regions, not between arbitrary neighboring regions.
- `AtmosphericUpdateScheduler` does not diffuse pressure between regions. It targets each region's own forecast pressure and base pressure.

## 7. Systems That Recover Pressure

- Scheduler forecast recovery toward `getTargetPressure(dayTime)`.
- Scheduler pressure guard when deviation from target exceeds the guard threshold.
- Scheduler base relaxation toward `basePressure`.
- Seasonal drift toward `targetPressure + seasonalPressureOffset`.
- Rain pressure recovery while rain intensity is present.
- Ocean basin pressure coupling can behave like recovery only if basin target pressure is closer to normal than live pressure.
- Cyclone pressure itself has no explicit recovery step after the cyclone exits; recovery is left to the scheduler, base relaxation, seasonal drift, wind, and ocean coupling.

## 8. Cyclone Pressure Lifecycle Analysis

Cyclones are support-gated and cooldown-gated. A cyclone can spawn only when nearby candidate regions have strong humidity, cloud water, thunderstorm support, and supercell support.

Active cyclone pressure lowering is capped:

- `maxPressureDrop = min(8, corePressureDrop)`
- `currentDrop = max(0, targetPressure - livePressure)`
- `allowedDrop = lerp(scaledInfluence, 12, 38)`
- `remainingDrop = max(0, allowedDrop - currentDrop)`
- `pressureDelta = -min(remainingDrop, pressurePulse)`

This means cyclone pressure forcing stops once the region has already reached its allowed deficit. The cyclone system does not include its own pressure rebound after the cyclone weakens or moves away. That is intentional separation of responsibilities, but it means unsupported post-cyclone lows depend on scheduler/base/season/wind/ocean recovery.

## 9. WeatherCell Pressure Feedback Analysis

WeatherCells do not call `adjustPressure` or `setPressure`.

They do use pressure indirectly:

- `AtmosphericSupportEvaluator` turns low pressure into cloud birth, rain formation, storm, and low-pressure support values.
- `WeatherCellFormationController` records `pressureAnomaly = 1013.25 - support.pressure()` on newly created cells.
- `WeatherCellLifecycleController` refreshes the cell pressure anomaly from current support.

Feedback exists, but it is indirect:

- low pressure increases WeatherCell support;
- WeatherCells increase rain/cloud cover and drain cloud water;
- rain then raises pressure through scheduler rain pressure recovery.

No direct WeatherCell low-pressure write was found.

## 10. Ocean Flux / Ocean Pressure Analysis

Ocean humidity flux can be `0.0` near an ocean when:

- ocean basin detection is not ready;
- no basin exists because no region has an ocean dominant biome;
- the sampled player region is near an ocean visually but is not an ocean cell or adjacent influence cell in `influenceWeights`;
- the region's dominant biome is not classified as ocean;
- the current backend or world state has not rebuilt basin influence weights yet.

Ocean influence is not only humidity. `AtmosphereFluxInfluence` also moves pressure toward `basin.getBasePressure() + basin.getPressureOffset()` with coupling `0.0025 * weight`.

This pressure influence is not currently gated by cyclone or storm support. It is slow, but if basin base pressure is biased low, nearby active ocean/coastal regions can remain lower than expected. This is not changed in this pass; `/pa debug verify` now reports `Ocean Flux` and `Ocean Pressure Influence` so the next pressure pass can confirm whether this is materially contributing.

## 11. Forecast Pressure / Target Pressure Analysis

Forecast pressure is generated from:

- altitude barometric baseline;
- average forecast temperature;
- computed air density from temperature/humidity;
- small random variation;
- clamp to `900..1080 hPa`.

Live `RegionAtmosphereState` starts at average forecast pressure. `getTargetPressure(dayTime)` uses the region's daily forecast profile. `basePressure` is the average forecast baseline and is persisted indirectly through region forecast data, while mutable live pressure is persisted by `AtmosphericStateSavedData`.

Important distinction:

- `ForecastOrchestrator.getCurrentPressure(ServerLevel, BlockPos, tick)` returns forecast pressure.
- `ForecastOrchestrator.getCurrentPressure(RegionInstanceKey, tick)` returns live pressure if live state exists.

That API difference can make pressure debugging confusing if callers mix position-based and region-key sampling.

## 12. Why Pressure Can Remain Low

Pressure can correctly remain low when:

- forecast target pressure is low;
- an active cyclone is still applying pressure forcing;
- ocean basin target pressure is below live pressure;
- wind mixing imports lower pressure from neighbors;
- seasonal drift target is below forecast target;
- active storm support remains strong enough to keep rain/cloud systems present.

Pressure can remain low longer than expected when:

- live pressure is below forecast but still not low enough to trigger large guard correction;
- base relaxation is weak by design;
- wind mixing repeatedly imports low pressure;
- ocean basin target pressure is low and active;
- no post-storm recovery gate exists beyond scheduler/base recovery.

The observed example `1007.9 hPa forecast`, `988.6 hPa live`, delta `-19.3 hPa` is inside the intended stormy range if there is real rain/storm/cyclone/ocean/season support. It is suspicious only if `Pressure Recovery Eligible` is true and support diagnostics are all weak.

## 13. Whether Current Behavior Is Correct Or Biased Low

The code is not a simple global low-pressure runaway after the stabilization pass. However, it can still be biased low in specific contexts:

- Ocean pressure coupling can maintain low coastal pressure without storm gating.
- Wind mixing can import pressure deficits from neighboring active regions.
- Cyclone recovery is indirect after cyclone forcing stops.
- Low pressure is a support input for WeatherCells, so persistent low pressure can keep weather support elevated even without direct WeatherCell pressure writes.

This pass did not change pressure behavior. The new debug output is intended to identify which of those sources is responsible in a live case.

## 14. Risks Of Adding Pressure Recovery

Adding a blanket pressure normalization force would flatten pressure gradients and weaken natural bad weather formation.

Specific risks:

- rain and storm systems become too short-lived;
- cyclones lose their pressure identity;
- wind pressure gradients become meaningless;
- ocean/coastal moderation becomes hidden by a global correction;
- seasonal pressure effects become muted;
- neighboring low-pressure propagation stops working.

Any recovery should be conditional and very weak.

## 15. Proposed Minimal Pressure Improvement, If Needed

Do not implement until the new debug fields show unsupported low-pressure regions are common.

Recommended future design:

- add gentle post-storm pressure recovery only when `Pressure Recovery Eligible: yes`;
- maximum recovery `1..4 hPa` per Minecraft day;
- disable or heavily reduce when rain, high cloud water, WeatherCell support, cyclone influence, thunderstorm support, strong convergence, or ocean low-pressure influence is active;
- apply after direct storm/cyclone/ocean/wind effects so it does not fight supported systems.

This should recover stale unsupported lows without flattening active weather.

## 16. Season Drift Root Cause

Two season bugs were found:

- Serene Seasons stage was read correctly, but progress was computed as `state.getDay() / state.getSeasonDuration()`. Local Serene bytecode shows `getSeasonDuration()` is ticks and `getDay()` is total cycle day, so this ratio stays near zero or becomes invalid for within-season progress.
- Serene and Ecliptic delegates returned `temperatureOffset = 0.0f` for every stage. `/pa debug verify` displayed this neutral snapshot offset, so winter and spring showed `+0.0 C` even though `SeasonalAtmosphericDrift` had a private stage-based modifier.

Additional finding:

- Seasonal drift was active and applied every 200 ticks through `ForecastOrchestrator.tick`, not only on season-change events.
- The displayed offset was misleading because it showed snapshot offset, not effective drift offset.

## 17. Season Drift Fix

Implemented:

- Added `SeasonClimateProfile` as a shared season curve.
- Serene Seasons progress now uses `getSeasonCycleTicks() % getSeasonDuration()` divided by `getSeasonDuration()`, matching Serene's tick-based API.
- Serene and Ecliptic snapshots now include seasonal temperature offset.
- `SeasonalAtmosphericDrift` no longer also adds the same stage temperature through the private modifier, preventing double application.
- Effective live temperature drift magnitude remains consistent with the previous private modifier curve.
- Added read-only drift accessors for current temperature offset, snapshot temperature offset, pressure offset, and initialization state.

Expected debug effect:

- Winter snapshot temperature offset is negative.
- Spring snapshot temperature offset is positive.
- Season progress advances within the current season instead of staying at `0.00`.
- Drift temperature/pressure offsets are displayed separately.
