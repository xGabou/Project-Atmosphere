  What you were seeing

  That black blob is the new volumetric cloud pass drawing a giant cloud cell in
  front of the entire world. Your logs confirm the pipeline was fully live
  (shaders compiled at 14:02, noise textures ready at 14:04, no render
  exceptions), so it wasn't a load failure — it was rendering, just with broken
  depth.

  Root cause

  FullscreenQuad.draw() renders through vanilla's
  VertexBuffer.drawWithShader(new Matrix4f(), new Matrix4f(), shader). I
  verified against the decompiled 1.20.1 sources: _drawWithShader overwrites any
  uniform named ProjMat (vanilla's managed PROJECTION_MATRIX name) with the
  matrix you pass in — which was identity, since the fullscreen quad doesn't
  need matrices for positioning.

  So even though VolumetricCloudRenderer carefully uploaded the real projection
  into ProjMat, it was silently replaced with identity right before the draw.
  The raymarch shader's depthAt() then computed gl_FragDepth ≈ 0 (nearest
  possible) for every cloud pixel, and the composite pass — which depth-tests
  clouds against the scene — passed everywhere. Result: the cloud's silhouette
  painted over all terrain. The black center is the thick core of a cell
  overhead (sun scatter fully extinguished, only weak ambient), fading to gray
  at its soft edges where the fogged sky shows through.

  Fixes applied

  - Renamed the uniform to CloudProjMat in cloud_atmosphere_volume.fsh, its
  .json, and VolumetricCloudRenderer.java, so vanilla can no longer stomp it.
  Your other names (ViewRotMat, InvProjMat, InvViewRotMat, WorldTime,
  OutputSize) are safe — I checked them all against vanilla's managed list.
  - Clamped ambient *= max(1.0 - localDensity * 0.35, 0.0) in the shader — with
  the funnel smooth-union, density can exceed ~2.9 and this term went negative
  (negative radiance = pushes toward black).

  compileJava passes with only pre-existing warnings.

  Things to watch after the fix (not changed)

  - Undersides of thick cells will still be very dark at ULTRA — the
  multi-scatter octaves decay fast (a *= 0.42) and storm darkening halves
  ambient. If it reads too black in daylight, the tuning knobs are the octave
  falloff in sampleLighting and the ambient floor.
  - The 0.88 temporal history blend re-samples the history bilinearly every
  frame, which will progressively smooth away noise detail. Worth revisiting
  once the picture is right.
  - Failure mode gap: one render exception sets runtimeEnabled = false for the
  session, but CloudFieldVolumeRenderHook checks isActive() (config only) — so
  the legacy fallback stays dormant too and you'd get no clouds until restart.
  Fine for debugging, but you may want isActive() to reflect the runtime kill
  switch.
  - The two log warnings about OutputSize/NightFactor are harmless — the GLSL
  compiler optimized out unused uniforms.

  If clouds still look wrong after restarting,
  VolumetricCloudRenderHook.status() (exposed through your telemetry/cloud debug
  command) reports lastStatus, cell counts, and raymarch GPU time — that's the
  quickest way to see which stage misbehaves next.
